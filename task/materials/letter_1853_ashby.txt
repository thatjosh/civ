Long Croft Farm, Hollowmere
12 October 1853

My dear Margaret,

You will have heard in Netherby of our misfortune. On the night of the fourth the mill took fire, nobody knows how, though William says a lamp left in the dressing room is the likeliest cause. By the time Matthew Carrow had the bell rung the roof was gone. The wheel and both stones are lost, and the whole of the machinery with them. Thank God no one was hurt. Josiah Reeve, who is miller there and has been these twenty years, went in twice for his books and came out with the ledgers under his coat, which Edmund Dunmore says was foolish and brave in equal parts.

We have no mill now nearer than yours at Netherby, and the carting will cost us dear this winter. A collection was taken on Sunday for the Reeves. Josiah is much shaken and his son Samuel has taken the running of what is left.

Write soon and tell me the price of grinding at Netherby, for we shall be sending John over with the cart.

Your loving sister,
Ruth Ashby
